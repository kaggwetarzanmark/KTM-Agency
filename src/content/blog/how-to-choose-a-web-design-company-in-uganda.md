There are dozens of web designers and agencies in Uganda. Some are excellent. Some will take your money, build something unusable and disappear. Knowing how to tell the difference before you sign anything can save you a lot of time and money.

This guide walks you through exactly what to look for, what to ask and what warning signs to avoid.

## 1. Look at Their Portfolio First

The single most important thing to check is their previous work. Any credible web design company in Uganda should be able to show you:

- At least 5 completed websites
- Live URLs you can visit and test on your phone
- Work across different industries

When reviewing their portfolio, ask yourself:
- Do the sites load quickly on mobile?
- Do they look professional and modern?
- Is there a clear call-to-action on each site?
- Can I find these businesses on Google?

If they can't show you a portfolio, or the sites load slowly and look outdated, that tells you everything you need to know.

## 2. Check If They Understand SEO

A beautiful website that nobody can find on Google is a wasted investment. Before hiring any web design company in Uganda, ask these questions directly:

- "Do you do keyword research before building the site?"
- "How do you handle on-page SEO — title tags, meta descriptions, headings?"
- "Will you set up my Google Business Profile?"
- "Will the site appear in Google Search Console?"

A good agency will have clear answers. A bad one will use vague language like "we make your site SEO-friendly" without being able to explain what that means.

## 3. Understand What's Included

Many Ugandan web design quotes look cheap until you see what isn't included. Before you compare prices, get clarity on:

**What's in the price:**
- Domain registration (first year)?
- Hosting setup and configuration?
- SSL certificate (HTTPS)?
- Mobile responsive design?
- Content upload?
- SEO setup?
- Training to update the site yourself?
- Post-launch support?

Get everything in writing. A quote without a written scope of work is an invitation for disputes.

## 4. Ask About Their Process

A professional web design company will have a defined process. Ask them to walk you through it:

1. How do they start a project? (discovery call, brief, questionnaire?)
2. How do they handle revisions?
3. What do they need from you and when?
4. What's the timeline from start to launch?
5. Who is your main point of contact?

Vague answers to these questions signal a disorganised operation.

## 5. Check Their Communication

How quickly do they respond to your first enquiry? This predicts how they'll communicate during the project and after launch. In Uganda's market, WhatsApp responsiveness is a key indicator — a team that takes 3 days to reply to a sales enquiry will take longer to fix a post-launch issue.

## 6. Ask About Hosting

Where your website is hosted matters enormously for speed in Uganda. Ask:

- "Where will my website be hosted?"
- "What is the uptime guarantee?"
- "How fast does it load on a Ugandan mobile connection?"

The best agencies in Uganda use Cloudflare Pages or similar global CDNs that serve content from the nearest server to your visitor. A site hosted on a slow shared server in Uganda or a remote data centre will load slowly on MTN and Airtel connections.

## 7. Understand the Maintenance Arrangement

Websites need ongoing attention — security updates, content changes, bug fixes. Ask:

- "What happens when something breaks after launch?"
- "Do you offer a maintenance retainer?"
- "Can I update the site myself, and will you train me?"

Agencies that disappear after launch leave you stuck when problems arise.

## 8. Compare Proposals Properly

When comparing quotes from different agencies, make sure you're comparing the same things. A UGX 1.5M quote that includes everything is better value than a UGX 800K quote that charges separately for hosting, SSL and mobile design.

Create a checklist and score each agency on:
- Portfolio quality
- SEO knowledge
- What's included in the price
- Communication speed
- Clarity of process
- Post-launch support

## Red Flags to Avoid

- No written contract or scope of work
- No portfolio or only mockup screenshots (not live sites)
- Can't explain how they'll get your site on Google
- Promises first-page ranking in 1 week (this is not how SEO works)
- No clarity on who owns the domain and hosting after the project
- Asks for full payment upfront before any work begins

## Questions to Ask Before Signing

1. Can I see 5 websites you've built that are live right now?
2. What is your exact process from brief to launch?
3. What's included in the quote — domain, hosting, SSL, SEO, content?
4. Who owns the domain and hosting account after launch?
5. How do I reach you if something breaks after the site goes live?
6. Can I update the site myself?
